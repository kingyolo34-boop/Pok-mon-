# Pokémon Fallen Ashe — Story Bible v1: THE ASH CROWN

> **Status:** Narrative draft for implementation. The attack on Mom, starter choice, Rook killing her, immediate battle, lifelong pursuit, the five legendary Pokémon, Wren, and ruined Ashvale are established requirements. The Ash Crown / Eclipse Mark explanation below is the proposed connective lore; revise freely before final event scripting.

## The central promise
**"They didn't burn Cinderfall to conquer it. They came to kill the one person who could save Ashvale."**

The Danes are a militarized regime who built **the Ash Crown**, a machine that feeds on two imprisoned legendary powers. **Smokerdile** provides the burning energy, **Shadowvile** the darkness to bind and control Pokémon. Each town that opposes the Danes is starved into obedience, or reduced to ash. The machine caused much of Ashvale's collapse. Their public message is that it is the only way to restore order.

There is one thing their machine cannot override: **the Eclipse Mark**, a bond bestowed by the hidden legendary **Veylith**, the living eclipse. The protagonist was marked as a child during the night the sky broke. The Mark lets the protagonist **break the Crown's hold on the legendaries and free the region**; no machine and no other trainer can duplicate this bond.

Mom knew the secret because she once helped the Danes decipher ancient Ashvale records before learning what the Crown was really for. She stole the **Keeper's Seal**, a small relic containing directions to the old guardian sanctuaries and proof of the Danes' atrocities. She escaped with her child and went into hiding in Cinderfall. She is brave, resourceful and morally complicated—she helped open the wrong door, then spent years trying to close it. The Seal alone is not enough to stop the Crown. The Mark belongs **only to the player**.

**The Danes' plan:** kill Mom to destroy the last witness and seize the Seal; kill the protagonist because they are the only person capable of stopping the Crown. They do not intend to adopt the protagonist or make them a weapon. They want the threat erased.

**Why Rook never stops:** Rook personally leads the strike and kills Mom, but fails the order to kill the child. His commanders make the failure his responsibility. His pursuit grows from a military assignment into a personal obsession: he cannot accept being defeated by the person he was ordered to destroy.

## Opening: Cinderfall — the night of the attack (playable Chapter One)
Cinderfall is already a wounded settlement: boarded houses, ashfall, burned rooftops, makeshift relief clinic, ration depot, and survivors hiding what little they have. The Dane attack is the final blow to an already devastated place.

1. **The warning.** Mom and the player are traveling toward the outskirts to retrieve medicine/supplies. A scorched Dane insignia hangs from the gate. Mom recognizes an approaching patrol and tries to turn back.
2. **The ambush.** Three Dane soldiers block the road. They call Mom a traitor, demand the Keeper's Seal, and order the child seized or killed. Mom buys seconds by throwing her bag into the path.
3. **The three Poké Balls.** Three Poké Balls tumble into the dirt. Mom tells the player to choose **Verdrake** (Grass Dragon), **Aqualye** (Water Unicorn), or **Embergryph** (Fire Griffin). Each Pokémon is an original surviving guardian-bred starter from the hidden sanctuary she protected.
4. **Rook.** A Dane leader steps forward after the starter is chosen. Rook recognizes the player from old intelligence reports. Mom refuses to surrender them. He kills Mom in a dramatic but non-graphic cutscene; her last words instruct the player to protect others, not become like him.
5. **The first battle.** The player immediately fights Rook, whose early-game partner is a low-level **Poochyena**. Rook withdraws when the road collapses/other survivors distract his patrol. This is a temporary escape, not a decisive defeat of his army.
6. **Sanctuary.** A survivor leads the player to the battered **Relief Center** in Cinderfall. A note left by Mom says, "Find the last guardians. They remember what the Danes tried to bury." A small broken-seal symbol flashes briefly on the player's wrist. **Wren**, the center's upstairs trainer, offers healing and a future repeatable Blissey training battle.

## Story structure

### Act I — The Hunted (opening / early gyms)
The protagonist escapes Cinderfall with their first Pokémon. Dane patrols post sketches of a young trainer with an eclipse-shaped mark, blockade bridges, and question villagers. Helping a starving settlement makes hiding harder but earns allies. Rook reappears at an abandoned road checkpoint, cornering the protagonist after a gym. The first fragment of Mom's research suggests the Crown has *two living power sources* and **five ancient guardians** matter to the old world.

**Player emotion:** fear turns to determination. **Mechanic:** early towns provide shelter or information after helping them; Wren gives a reliable place to train.

### Act II — The Last Guardians of Cinderfall (roughly gym 3–4)
At the **Lastwood survivor shelter**, the Danes attack civilians searching for the Keeper's Seal. Two legendary canine guardians intervene:

- **Deku** — Fighting/Steel. Disciplined, protective, deliberate; battles the protagonist to test whether they defend the helpless. Signature **Guardian Crush**; ability **Warpath** raises Attack and Defense after each knockout.
- **Asta** — Normal/Fighting. Huge, chaotic, affectionate, battle-hungry, with his distinctive "wooo" call. Charges Dane armored transports and breaks open a path to free captives; signature **Meat Missile** and ability **Rushdown** raises Speed when struck.

Both are **living, unique legendary Pokémon** with heroic roles, not ghosts or stand-ins for dead pets. Their bond is built through rescuing people; they later become available to join the player's team through the guardian quest. Rook's ambush makes this rescue a defining moment: the protagonist chooses to help people even when fleeing would be safer.

**Player emotion:** the lone survivor finds a family of allies.

### Act III — The Two Prisoners (middle / late gyms)
The player uncovers ruined experimental laboratories. **Smokerdile** (Fire/Poison) is chained to the Crown's furnace, poisoning the skies with its forced flames. **Shadowvile** (Dark/Ghost) is trapped in a containment network that spreads fear across towns. Neither is inherently evil. Breaking their restraints weakens the Danes, but causes dangerous surges: storms, burned routes and mass panic. The player works with Deku, Asta and survivors to save both legendaries instead of destroying them.

Rook becomes increasingly brutal and reckless, abandoning some Dane orders to hunt the player. He realizes the stories about the Eclipse Mark were true. He also discovers Mom tried to stop the original catastrophe—and that the Danes concealed their own role in it. He does not suddenly become friendly or forgiven.

**Player emotion:** learning that the "monsters" were victims, too.

### Act IV — The Broken Eclipse (final gyms / endgame)
The Keeper's Seal opens a forgotten sanctuary. The third world legendary, **Veylith** (Ghost/Fairy), is revealed as the living eclipse: it marked the player years ago when Mom shielded them from the Crown's first eruption. **The player alone can break the system's bond**, but only after winning the trust of the other legendary guardians. The Danes' leaders activate the full Ash Crown to control every Pokémon in Ashvale and prepare to execute the protagonist on sight. Rook makes one last stand at the approach to the Crown. **Canon: after the final battle, the protagonist personally kills Rook. Rook does not survive, escape, or receive a redemption. That irreversible choice is the instant the protagonist understands revenge was not the answer.**

At the finale, the player and their Pokémon confront the Dane command. Shadowvile and Smokerdile are freed, the Ash Crown loses its power, and the sky brightens for the first time in years. **The protagonist saves Ashvale not because the Mark makes them invincible, but because they found allies who chose to stand together.**

**Player emotion:** protecting life, not living only for revenge.

### Epilogue — Cinderfall Rises
The ruins do not magically become pristine. Survivors rebuild fences, the Relief Center becomes a true sanctuary, and people bring supplies back to the trade depot. The protagonist returns to Mom's home and reads one final letter. **Rook is permanently dead at the protagonist's hand**; the hero cannot undo that choice, and saving Ashvale does not erase it. The final letter urges him to protect others rather than let vengeance define the rest of his life. The last scene shows fresh green growth breaking through the ash.

## Repeated encounters: Rook is a hunter, not a regular rival
- **Route 101:** kills Mom and battles player's newly chosen starter.
- **First checkpoint:** tracks player from reports; attempts to trap them, triggering a battle.
- **Lastwood:** tries to capture the guardians while the player rescues refugees.
- **Laboratory:** confronts player after discovering what the Ash Crown truly is; chooses pursuit again.
- **Crown approach:** final confrontation; the player defeats Rook's Pokémon, then kills Rook in a scripted, non-graphic aftermath. The realization is immediate and hollow, not triumphant.

Rook's team should evolve and get harder; the opening Poochyena is his earliest partner, not his permanent only Pokémon.

## Side content that must remain connected
- **Wren's Relief Center Blissey farm:** upstairs, repeatable trainer fight, Struggle only, 2× battle EXP and free healing, with scaling levels; quest tracked as GitHub issue #1. Gameplay system, not a replacement for the main plot.
- **Random Egg after each gym:** NPC outside offers one legally selectable species egg, hatches after 50 steps. This makes each milestone feel like survivors sharing hope.
- **Seven Cinderfall roles:** player house, scared family, angry survivor, abandoned note, supply-quest house, Relief Center, Trade Depot. These NPCs can react to the protagonist's progress.

## Canon extension: the protagonist's fall and redemption

The protagonist is not a purely heroic chosen one. His grief over Rook murdering Mom turns into vengeance. The Danes exploit that anger, and a catastrophic middle-game choice at a Crown relay collapses the storm shields over Lastwood ("Black Rain"). He tries to rebuild trust, save refugees with Deku and Asta, but **he still kills Rook at their final confrontation**. The emptiness afterward is his genuine moment of recognition. His lasting redemption begins as he refuses the Crown's power, rescues others and lives with what he has done.

## Locked canon correction — Rook's final fate (November 2026)
- The protagonist **kills Rook himself after their final Pokémon battle**; Rook stays dead.
- This is **not a glorious, heroic finish**. There is a shocked silence, and the protagonist realizes killing Rook did not restore Mom or erase his grief.
- Rook receives no absolution; his atrocities still matter. The protagonist's act also has weight and consequences.
- Deku, Asta and his starter remain with the protagonist, but their reactions show this was not the way to heal.
- From that moment, the protagonist's redemption is an ongoing commitment to save others, starting by stopping the Ash Crown without becoming its next master.
